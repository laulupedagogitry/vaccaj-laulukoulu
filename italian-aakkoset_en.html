Italian alphabet and pronunciation

This guide provides pronunciation instructions for Italian letters, phonetic transcriptions, and example words. The notation follows the original Vaccaj Singing School material.

Key to phonetic symbols

Key to the phonetic transcription: phonetic symbols are enclosed in brackets [ ].

[z] = voiced s, as in “zoo”, “zero”, and “zebra”; [ʃ] = sibilant, as in “sheep”, “shine”, and “assure”; [ʒ] = voiced sibilant, as in “pleasure”, “leisure”, and “measure”; [ʎ] = li as in “million”; [ɲ] = ny as in “canyon”; [g] = g as in “get”, “glass”, and “great”; [w] = a short, unstressed u at the beginning of a syllable, except in non-Italian loanwords, as in “we”, “why”, and “well”.

“(long)” means that, in a stressed position, the vowel is lengthened. In this material, lengthening is not marked by [:], but by ['] before the stressed syllable: caro ['karo], fare ['fare]. If a vowel is followed by a double consonant or two different consonants, it is usually pronounced short: latte ['latte], corso ['corso], Gherardo [ge'rardo].

Note: As a rule, Italian words are stressed on the penultimate syllable.
